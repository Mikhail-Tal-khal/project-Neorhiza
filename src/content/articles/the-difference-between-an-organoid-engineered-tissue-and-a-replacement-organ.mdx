Regenerative medicine generates headlines that blend three very different achievements into one impression. Separating them takes two minutes and permanently improves how you read the news in this field.

## Organoids: remarkable research models

An organoid is a small, three-dimensional cell culture that self-organizes into structures resembling aspects of an organ. Organoids have become extraordinary research tools: they let scientists study development, disease and drug responses in human-derived cells, in a dish.

What organoids are **not**: complete organs. They lack full architecture, functioning vasculature, immune integration and the size and consistency of the organs they resemble. They are windows into biology — and valuable ones — not spare parts.

## Engineered tissue: constructed with intent

Engineered tissue describes constructs built with more deliberate design: cells combined with supportive materials and structures, often under controlled environmental conditions, to achieve defined, measurable functions. Engineered tissue can be larger and more controlled than organoids, and perfused constructs push further still.

What engineered tissue is **not**, at present: a replacement organ. Scale, integration, safety, consistency and long-term behavior remain open research questions. Every honest description of the field's state of the art says exactly this.

## Replacement organs: the long-term objective

A replacement organ would be a tissue construct able to sustain the functions of a failing organ in a patient's body — safely, durably and under the body's own regulation. Nothing available today meets this definition. The path toward it runs through decades of the first two categories: better models, better constructs, better evidence.

## Why the distinction protects patients

When headlines blur these categories, two harms follow. Public hope inflates beyond what the science supports, and bad actors find room to sell unproven interventions to desperate people. Precision is a safety tool.

Project Neorhiza's communication follows the same discipline: its first proposed program is a small perfused liver-tissue research model — engineered tissue research, explicitly. Its long-term objective — replacement-organ technologies — is labeled as exactly that: long-term, and reachable only through authorized institutions, ethics bodies and regulators.
