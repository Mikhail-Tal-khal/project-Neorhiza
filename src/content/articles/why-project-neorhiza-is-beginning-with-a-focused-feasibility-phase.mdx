Early-stage projects face a temptation: describe the destination in ever more vivid detail, and postpone the first hard experiment. Project Neorhiza is structured to resist that temptation, and this update explains how.

## The pattern we chose to avoid

Long-term visions attract two failure modes. The first is overpromising: a roadmap written as if success were scheduled rather than earned. The second is vagueness: a vision so general that no measurement could ever confirm or refute progress. Both damage credibility, and both ultimately harm the communities the work is meant to serve.

## What we are doing instead

Project Neorhiza's first proposed program — the Neorhiza Liver Tissue Program — is a small perfused liver-tissue research model with one focused research question:

> Can controlled perfusion improve the sustained function and viability of a small multicellular liver-tissue construct compared with an equivalent non-perfused construct?

The initial objective is explicitly **not** to manufacture a transplantable liver. It is to investigate how defined cell populations, tissue organization and controlled perfusion may work together to support measurable tissue function. Public measurement categories include cell survival, tissue organization, protein production, metabolic activity, transport behavior, reproducibility and process consistency.

## What the program requires

The program carries a status we repeat deliberately: **Proposed Program — Institutional Collaboration Required.** No laboratory work begins without qualified institutional supervision, ethics review and appropriate agreements. Detailed methodologies, engineering parameters and experimental procedures remain confidential and subject to institutional scientific review.

## Why this earns trust

A focused feasibility phase does three things at once. It produces real evidence instead of rhetorical momentum. It surfaces negative or inconclusive results early, when they are cheapest and most useful. And it demonstrates, in behavior rather than words, how the project intends to treat every later stage: with defined measurements, honest reporting and supervision before action.

We will report on this program's progress — including what does not work — on this page.
